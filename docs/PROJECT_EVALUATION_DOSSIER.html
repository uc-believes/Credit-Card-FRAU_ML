01 Foundational Knowledge: Fraud Machine Learning Demystified

Essential concepts explained in plain English to help you confidently present the project to any evaluator, panel, or stakeholder.

The Extreme Class Imbalance Problem

In our dataset of 284,807 transactions, exactly 284,315 are legitimate (99.828%) and only 492 are fraudulent (0.172%). That means only 1 out of every 578 transactions is an attack.

The "99.9% Accuracy" Trap: A naive model that simply predicts every transaction is legitimate will achieve 99.83% accuracy. But it is completely useless because it catches 0% of fraud. In imbalanced domains, raw accuracy is a vanity metric.

Precision vs. Recall vs. F2-Score

Standard metrics have direct banking consequences:

  • Recall: "Out of all stolen cards, what % did we catch?" High recall prevents millions in chargebacks.
  • Precision: "When we flag a transaction, what % are actually fraud?" High precision avoids declining legitimate customers at checkout.
  • F2-Score: A weighted harmonic mean that puts twice as much importance on Recall as Precision. In banking, missing a $2,000 fraud is far worse than spending $15 for an analyst to check a false alarm.

ROC-AUC vs. PR-AUC

ROC-AUC looks at True Positive Rate vs False Positive Rate. Because the number of legitimate transactions is huge (284,315), the False Positive Rate denominator is massive, making ROC-AUC deceptively high (~0.97).

PR-AUC plots Precision against Recall without using True Negatives. If false alarms spike, Precision drops immediately. PR-AUC is the true gold standard for rare-event detection.

Explainable AI (TreeSHAP) in Plain English

Machine learning models are often called "black boxes". If an investigator or regulator asks: "Why was this customer's card blocked?", answering "because tree #42 said so" is unacceptable.

SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) borrows mathematics from Nobel-prize-winning game theory to calculate the exact dollar/risk attribution of every single feature. The sum of all feature SHAP values equals the exact model score.

02 Phase-by-Phase Technical Implementation Journey

A chronological breakdown of every phase executed in the development of Fraud Shield.

Phase 1: Exploratory Analysis & Zero-Leakage Architecture

We began with the 284,807-row dataset containing 30 features: Time, Amount, and 28 anonymized PCA vectors (V1–V28).

Critical Engineering Decision: Enforced a strict split-first protocol (70% Train, 10% Validation, 20% Test). Scalers (RobustScaler for skewed amounts) were fitted strictly on the training partition to guarantee zero data leakage into test evaluations. Engineered log_amount, hour_of_day, and nocturnal attack indicators (is_night).

Phase 3: Explainable AI with Local & Global TreeSHAP

Integrated TreeSHAP into an automated inference service (src/explainability/). For every evaluated transaction, the system computes:

  • Global Feature Importance: Demonstrated that features V14, V12, V10, V4, and V11 account for over 65% of global fraud separation.
  • Individual Attributions: Live waterfall attributions detailing positive risk drivers and mitigating factors with signed Shapley values.
  • Auditor Disclaimer: Explicitly documented that feature attributions represent learned statistical correlations, not legal culpability.

Phase 4: Multi-Tier Risk Scoring & Decision Decoupling

Architected src/risk/engine.py to formally decouple:

  • Model Probability ($P \in [0.0, 1.0]$): Raw calibrated sigmoid probability.
  • Normalized Risk Score ($S \in [0, 100]$): Human-readable integer scale: Low Risk (0–30), Medium Risk (31–70), High Risk (71–100).
  • Business Decision: Configurable operational thresholding. Acceptance is explicitly documented as operational risk acceptance, never a guarantee of legitimacy.

Phase 6: Adaptive Fraud Intelligence & Cost Optimization

Formulated fraud detection as a financial optimization problem: 

$\min_{T \in [0, 1]} \text{Total Financial Loss}(T) = \sum_{i \in \text{FN}} \text{Amount}_i \cdot \omega_{\text{loss}} + \text{FP}(T) \cdot C_{\text{review}}$

Implemented an interactive slider in the UI showing how moving the threshold from 0.05 to 0.90 alters caught dollars, false alarms, and operational costs. Added live Population Stability Index (PSI) drift monitoring and multi-factor SLA queue prioritization (P1 - CRITICAL to P4 - LOW).

04 Evaluator Defense Guide: Top Questions & How to Answer Them

Anticipated questions from technical examiners and how to deliver authoritative, high-scoring answers.

Q1 Why did you select XGBoost over Random Forest or Deep Learning? ▾
Your Answer: "While Random Forest performed well (PR-AUC 0.8012), XGBoost outperformed all models across both PR-AUC (0.8178) and F2-Score (0.8134). Crucially for production payment rails, XGBoost delivers an ultra-fast inference latency of ~4 ms per transaction with an artifact size of only 436 KB. Deep learning models add immense latency, lack exact TreeSHAP speed optimizations, and offer no performance benefit on tabular PCA components."
Q2 Why did you avoid SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling)? ▾
Your Answer: "In Phase 2 we investigated SMOTE. In a high-dimensional PCA space with an extreme 0.172% imbalance, interpolating synthetic points between distant fraud cases creates artificial samples across legitimate decision spaces, inflating false alarms. We instead utilized cost-sensitive class weighting (scale_pos_weight = 578.0) combined with threshold optimization on the PR-curve, which preserves authentic distribution boundaries."
Q3 What does your optimal threshold of 0.2773 achieve? ▾
Your Answer: "A standard 0.50 threshold naively assumes that missing a fraud costs the same as a false alarm. In reality, letting a $2,000 fraud escape is vastly more expensive than spending $15 for an analyst review. Our threshold analysis on the validation distribution proved that lowering the decision cutoff to 0.2773 increases fraud recall from 85.11% to 86.17% while keeping precision above 91%, minimizing net financial loss."
Q4 How does SHAP guarantee mathematical exactness? ▾
Your Answer: "SHAP calculates Shapley values from cooperative game theory, determining the marginal contribution of each feature across all possible coalitions. Due to the property of exact additivity, the sum of all individual feature SHAP values equals the exact difference between the transaction's prediction and the base expected value: $f(x) = \mathbb{E}[f(x)] + \sum \phi_i$. It provides statistical attribution without black-box guessing."
Q5 How does your system prevent Data Leakage? ▾
Your Answer: "We enforce a strict split-first pipeline. The raw dataset was partitioned into 70% Train, 10% Validation, and 20% Test before any transformations occurred. All feature scaling parameters—such as the median and interquartile range for RobustScaler—were calculated strictly on the training set and frozen. The validation and test sets were transformed using only these pre-fitted parameters."
Q6 How do you monitor for concept drift in production? ▾
Your Answer: "We track the Population Stability Index (PSI) between the baseline validation score distribution and live inference streams. PSI < 0.10 indicates stable operations; 0.10 to 0.25 indicates moderate drift; and PSI >= 0.25 triggers an automated retraining alert. We also compute 2-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistics and Wasserstein distances across key features."
Q7 What security measures protect your API and dashboard? ▾
Your Answer: "During our hostile QA audit, we hardened the platform with defense-in-depth: the API strictly validates numeric types and rejects NaN, Infinity, negative values, and amounts > $10M with clean 400 Bad Request responses. Queue statuses are strictly whitelisted, notes are escaped using Python's html.escape(), client-side rendering uses an escapeHtml() sanitizer against DOM XSS, and HTTP headers enforce nosniff and SAMEORIGIN."
05 Future Scope, Scalability & Enterprise Roadmap

A forward-looking architectural roadmap showing how Fraud Shield scales to global banking environments.

Real-Time Velocity Features (Redis & Kafka)

The Kaggle dataset contains isolated transaction snapshots without cardholder IDs. In production banking feeds, Fraud Shield will integrate an in-memory Redis cluster to calculate sliding-window velocities:

  • card_swipe_count_10m: Swipes in last 10 minutes (detects card cloning).
  • card_spend_sum_1h: Dollar velocity in last 60 minutes.
  • merchant_fraud_ratio_24h: Risk score of acquiring terminal.

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for Collusion Rings

Modern fraudsters rarely act alone—they operate fraud rings sharing phone numbers, IP subnets, and shipping addresses.

By integrating Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) via PyTorch Geometric, Fraud Shield can uncover multi-account collusion networks that individual transaction-level classifiers cannot detect.
